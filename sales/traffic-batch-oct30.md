## 4. Community answer draft — r/handyman "Best managing apps" (Sep 22, 2026)
Thread: https://www.reddit.com/r/handyman/comments/1wmvauc/best_managing_apps/
Post as Ryan (disclose the link is his site):
Since you've got flat rate, T&M and quote-first jobs, the thing to test is whether the estimate turns into the invoice without retyping. That's where most of the cheap apps differ.

Rough way to split it for a one-man shop:
- Free to start: Wave does invoices and basic books at $0, Pro is $19/mo. Fine if you mostly just need to bill and track payments.
- Phone-first invoices only: Invoice Simple is quick to set up, but it's not accounting and the low tiers cap how many invoices you can send.
- Scheduling plus quotes plus invoices in one: Jobber or Housecall Pro are the usual picks, but you pay more. ServiceM8 has a free plan (1 user, 30 jobs a month) if you're on iPhone.
- If phones are the real pain (missing calls on a ladder), a cheap AI answering service like Dialzara (from $29/mo) or Upfirst (from $24.95 for 30 calls) is a separate add-on.

Square or your bank's tap-to-pay is fine for getting paid on site, it just won't track quotes or jobs.

## 5. Community answer draft — r/smallbusiness "Home service owners: Are you guys actually using AI voice receptionists, or do they just piss off older customers?"
Thread: https://www.reddit.com/r/smallbusiness/comments/1ulegrs/home_service_owners_are_you_guys_actually_using/
(Check the thread isn't archived before posting.)
Post as Ryan:
The older-customer worry is real, but from what I've seen comparing these tools it's mostly about setup, not the tech. The ones that annoy people are the ones that won't let you reach a person, or that take a message and nobody calls back.

What seems to work for trades shops:
- Have it say up front it's an assistant, and always offer "press 0 or say 'person'" to get transferred.
- Send real emergencies (no heat, active leak, gas smell) straight to the on-call cell, and let the AI handle routine booking and price questions.
- Before you commit, make 10 test calls yourself during the free trial: a mumbling older caller, a bad cell connection, someone who only wants a price. You'll know in 10 minutes.
- Watch the billing model. Per-minute plans can jump in your busy month; some (like NextPhone) are flat unlimited, others (Rosie, OnCrew) start around $49/mo with caps.

A lot of shops use it only after hours and on overflow, so regulars still get a human during the day.
